01 The idea

Three tripwires, one alarm.

Each piece is a decoy with no real purpose, so anything that touches it is a warning worth hearing.

01

The decoys

A fake "easy target": a phony login, an open-looking admin panel, a database that answers. One lives on a small box we run; where it makes sense, one sits inside your own office network. That inside one matters most.

02

The alert

The moment something serious touches a decoy, your phone buzzes: who, what they tried, how bad. Flood-proof: one attacker can't page you 500 times, and an attacker can't switch it off.

03

The report

Once a month, one page in plain English: who came knocking, what they were after, what to do about it, and what we don't see. A quiet month says "nothing touched these doors", never "you're secure".

05 Said plainly

What we won't pretend.

Early access

Watchpost is in early access with no marketing push. We're taking on a small number of businesses so we can learn from real decoys at real clients before we say anything louder than this page.

How we compare, honestly

Checked 2026-09-21 against the vendors' own pages: Thinkst Canary does decoys better than we do and costs roughly ten times more; Huntress, Todyl, Guardz and Defendify sell 24/7 detection and response with endpoint software, which we don't; Microsoft Defender for Business (CAD $4.10/user/month) protects your computers, which we don't. Watchpost is the alarm on the fake doors, at a price a one-location business can approve without a meeting. Keep your antivirus.

What Watchpost is, and isn't

It's detection, not a lock. It tells you fast when someone's poking at doors they shouldn't, so you can act. It doesn't by itself stop a determined attacker, it doesn't replace passwords, updates, backups and a firewall, and no service on earth makes a business unhackable. It only watches decoys we placed, reports what it sees, and never touches or strikes back at anyone. Ask and we'll show you exactly how the decoys work; there's nothing hidden in them.

06 Questions

Questions people ask.

I've got nothing worth stealing.

Your card terminal, your staff's logins, your bank connection, and your reputation on Google when customers find out. Attackers don't pick targets; their scripts try every address on the internet. The report shows you how many tried yours.

My IT guy or MSP already handles security.

Keep them. This gives them one thing they usually don't have: a tripwire that only rings when something is actually wrong. We'll send them the report too, or they can run it for you on the Partner plan.

Isn't this just antivirus?

No. Antivirus watches your computers for bad files. This watches for someone hunting for doors. They're different jobs, and most businesses have the first and not the second.

Has it caught anything?

On our own box it caught internet scanners within hours, which is normal for anything online. We'll always tell you the truth about what it has and hasn't seen at real clients; that's the whole point of the report.

Is someone watching my alerts around the clock?

No, and we won't pretend otherwise. Alerts go straight to your phone. Dan reads your monthly report and answers email in business hours. If you need a team on call 24/7, that's a managed detection and response provider; Watchpost can send its alerts to them too.

What do you do with the data?

We keep the decoy logs (address, time, what was sent, cleaned to plain text) to build your report. We never scan, contact or "hack back" the addresses we see, and we never put anything on your real computers.
